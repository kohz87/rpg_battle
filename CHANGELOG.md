# Changelog

## 0.5.4

- **What next? after a fight.** The result screen offers a few things your character could do next instead of only *Return to story*: one short model call writes them from the hand-off and the scene when model calls are on (15-second limit, the Generated words model); off, offline, or when the call fails or says too little, code writes them from the result (skin or search the fallen, tend a hurt or downed companion, follow foes that fled, get clear after fleeing, come to after a defeat, another way to a stage not reached). A click puts one in the message box, Shift-click sends it; the reply tells the fight, then your move, and the hand-off is unchanged. *Just continue* sends the usual message. None after your character's death. Setting *Next moves after a fight* (Blocks), on by default.

## 0.5.3

- **Threat in plain levels, never tiers.** With tier titles on, a threat step was a whole tier: a *strong* foe for a Soul Class I (Lv 1) party was Soul Class II (Lv 11), and *deadly* two tiers up. A step is now always 4 plain levels: *strong* is +4, *deadly* +8, *weak* −4, *trivial* −8, with or without tiers.
- **Fixed bands for XP.** The level gap (foe − member) falls in a band with a fixed factor, instead of a smooth ×2 per 4 levels: +8 or more deadly ×4 (40 XP), +4 to +7 strong ×2 (20), −3 to +3 even ×1 (10), −4 to −7 weak ×½ (5), −8 or less trivial ×¼ (3). `xp.bands` and `xp.below` in rules; `minFactor`/`maxFactor` are gone.
- **A foe with a level of its own shows its threat.** A bestiary creature, a planned fight or a row with a written level gets the band its gap to your character is, on the setup and its fight card ("Lv 9 · deadly").

## 0.5.2

XP counted per level, an XP rate setting, threat words that work at low levels, and kin.

- **Every level needs 100 XP.** `character.xpExponent` is 0 by default (it was 1.5: 100 for level 2, 280 for level 3, 1120 from 5 to 6, 11 760 from 24), so `xpToNext` is `xpBase`, 100, at every level. Both stay settable in `rules.json`, and every gain is still a share of `xpToNext`, so a curve set there keeps working. A level is 100 XP, 4 points and the rest carried over, for companions too. A Party block written under the old curve is not converted: its XP reads as its first number, kept below 100 (below).

- **Fight XP is a share of each member's next level.** A defeated foe is worth a tenth (`rules.xp.even`, 0.10) of the XP the member needs for their next level when it is even, ×2 for each threat step it stands above them and ×½ for each below (a step is `threat.step`, 4 levels, or one tier with tier titles: *strong* ×2, *deadly* ×4, *weak* ×½, *trivial* ×¼), that factor within `xp.minFactor` 0.1 and `xp.maxFactor` 4; ×3 for a boss (`boss.xp`), half for a parley (`parley.xpFactor`); rounded per foe, at least 1. Each member counts every foe against their own level, nothing is split. With every level needing 100 that is 10 for an even foe at any level, 5 weak, 3 trivial, 20 strong, 40 deadly, 30 for an even boss, 5 for a parley (× the XP rate). The rank table's `xp` no longer counts in a fight (it is kept on the table and the Combatant for the record); `xp.perLevelAbove` and `xp.perLevelBelow` are gone, and `rules.xpFactor` is the new factor (`combat.foeXpWorth` the per-foe worth). Fights against foes well above you pay much more than before (up to ×4 instead of ×1.5), and those well below less.
- **XP rate** (*Settings → Party*, 1 by default, 0.25 to 5 in steps of 0.05; `rules.xpRate`): multiplies all XP, fights (each foe), parleys, `<XP>` story awards (10/25/50) and Story Director's quests (5/10/15/25), rounded, at least 1 when there was any. The offer, the result screen, the hand-off and the setup show the multiplied amount. It is read when a fight ends, so a fight under way when it changes counts at the new rate.
- **Progress is per level, also with tier titles.** With tiers on, progress was the share through the whole tier (`Soul Growth: 0.21/100`) and gains a share of the tier (`+0.2%`), so the bar beside *Soul Class I (Lv 1)* barely moved. Now it is XP within the level against XP to the next one, as without tiers (`Soul Growth: 30/100`), and gains read `+30` everywhere: the result screen, the hand-off's XP line, the offers and quest awards, the Party card's bar and the Party block's level line. The level label keeps the tier. The text is whole numbers only; `xpFromProgress` reads any value as its first number, rounded and kept within the level (0 to its need − 1): `30/100` is 30, an odd `45.20/100` 45, `140/280` 99, so nothing levels up from being read. The tier-share reading is gone.
- **Threat words at low levels.** *Weak* and *trivial* came out at level 1 for a party of level 1 to 4 (and *trivial* up to 8), the same numbers and XP as *even*. Now a foe the word would put below level 1 stays at level 1 and is scaled down: HP and attack × `threat.perLevelBelowOne` (0.88) per level short of 1 (counted in plain steps with tier titles), never below `threat.minScale` (0.35), defence unchanged; its XP counts from the level the word meant. A level-1 party meets a *weak* wolf with 10 HP (even 16) worth 5 XP (even 10), a *trivial* one with 6 HP worth 3. Only a row whose level came from the threat word is scaled; a written level, rank or power never is. Plans and waves carry it (`story.threatPower`; rows and Combatants carry `scale` and `xpLevel`).
- **A foe's level on its fight card.** Fight cards showed the rank letter (*F · skirmisher*) unless custom terms were on; now the level and the story's threat word: *Lv 1 · weak · skirmisher* (*Soul Power 1 · weak · skirmisher*, with tiers *Soul Class I · weak · skirmisher*). Boss and *new* badges as before. The setup shows each line's level the same way (*Lv 3* rather than *Rank E*; with custom terms as before) and **what one foe is worth to you**, by the result's formula at your XP rate: *~5 XP each*, *~30 XP* for one.
- **Monster families: kin.** A new kind in a family the story already has (the story knows the *Feral Ditch-Dog*; now it writes *razor dog*, *wolf dog* or *hellhound*) was made up as a plain template creature with nothing of its relative. Now it is made as **kin** of the closest relative (`templates.findKin`): the same creature noun first, then the same family (`templates.FAMILIES`: canine, feline, ursine, rodent, arachnid, serpent, avian, insect, ooze; any other noun its own family; the noun as classify reads it, a plural or a compound's ending: *ditch-dog*, *hellhound*, *wolfdog*), the story's own creatures before the shipped ones, the newest first. Kin takes the relative's template, role (unless its name has a role word or the row gives one), temperament and traits (with its own name's traits and leadership), talks and coins, its moves first (the template fills up to its count) and its loot with the body parts renamed (*Razor Dog Pelt*, *Razor Dog Fang*; gear valued again at its level, its rank's potion); the 0.5.0 caps still apply when it drops. Its level, numbers and XP are its own row's. Entries keep `kin` (the relative's name) and `family`; `cleanCreature` keeps them, and `coins`. The flavour prompt says *a relative of the Feral Ditch-Dog*; Regenerate keeps a kin a kin. An exact or close (0.5.1) match always wins: such a name is never made. With no relative, nothing changes. The Party tab's Bestiary fold groups the story's creatures by family (*Canines*, *Wights*…) and shows *kin of Feral Ditch-Dog*; the setup's *new* tag says *made up as kin of …*.

## 0.5.1

- **The bestiary's creatures are used, not remade.** A creature was found only by its exact name or an alias, and models word the same animal differently each time ("feral ditch-dog", "mangy ditch-dog", "young wolf"), so every new wording was made up as a new creature with its own numbers. Now a name that is not known exactly is matched by its words without the describing ones (young, old, mangy, feral, grey, alpha, matriarch…): it is the creature whose name or alias has the same remaining words, the one sharing most of the written words, the story's own on a tie. "young wolf" is the Wolf, "mangy ditch-dogs" the story's Feral Ditch-Dog, "wolf matriarch" its Grey Wolf Matriarch. A word that names a kind still counts, so "Dire Wolf", "Bog Wight" and "quill-dog" stay creatures of their own. The fight shows the story's wording; the setup no longer tags such a name *new*.
- **The model is told which creatures it has met.** The story instruction ends with *Foes this story has met (the same creature keeps its name): …*, the last eight creatures in this story's bestiary (about 5 to 25 tokens; none while the bestiary is empty). A name a model mangled with a colon (from before 0.5.0) is left out.

## 0.5.0

Plans: fights in stages. The story writes the plan; you choose only whether to start it and, between stages, whether to go on.

- **The syntax:** in the same `<Encounter>` block, a place after `then` starts a new stage, a separate fight somewhere else: `<Encounter>at the gate: 2 guard (weak); then at the yard: 3 guard (even); then at the tower: guard captain (boss, strong) | to the death</Encounter>`. `at the gate:` before the first group names stage 1 (optional; without it, *Stage 1*); `then, at the yard:` and `and then at the yard:` work too, any case. A plain `then:` is still a wave of the same fight, so a stage may have waves of its own. Up to five stages; a stage that names no foe is left out. `| to the death` applies to the whole plan. An encounter without stage marks is read exactly as before (`story.parsePlan`; `parseEncounter` rows carry `stage` only for a plan). Story Director's planned fights go through the same parser, so they may be plans too.
- **Offer and setup:** *A plan in 3 stages: the gate, the yard, the tower* (*— to the death* in the toast when marked); the setup lists *Stage 1 of 3: the gate* and so on, each with its foes as for any fight (its waves under it), read-only, **Start** or **Not now**. The fight is built from the offer RPG Battle parsed, as before.
- **Each stage is its own fight** (the same engine): new foes, rounds from one. HP, MP, cooldowns, conditions and the items left carry over from the stage before; nothing heals and whoever is down stays down. Each member starts every stage under its own control again (Auto in one stage does not play the next).
- **Between stages:** *The gate is cleared (3 rounds). Next: the yard — 3 guard (even)*, the party as it stands and the next stage's foes, and only **Go on** or **Fall back**. Kept in chat state: a reload finds the plan where it was, mid-stage or between stages.
- **Endings:** every stage won is victory; a stage lost ends the plan there in defeat, a stage fled from ends it fled, Fall back ends it withdrawn; later stages never happen.
- **Rewards:** what each cleared stage gave is kept, whatever comes after: XP for the foes beaten in every stage fought (also in the one lost or fled, as after one fight), the loot of every stage won, skill and item uses, applied once by the single fight's rules (levels, companions' auto-spend, the Inventory) when the plan ends, into the latest reply. Losing any stage of a plan to the death is a fight lost to the death (your sheet reads *dead*; companions down at the end fall). The result screen lists each stage (*Stage 1, the gate: victory in 3 rounds*, *Stage 3, the tower: not reached*, after Fall back *not attempted*) above the totals; its title is *Fell back* after a withdrawal.
- **The hand-off:** the usual header, a line per stage reached (*Stage 1, the gate: victory in 3 rounds.*, *Stage 2, the yard: defeated — the story decides what follows (captured, robbed, left for dead).*), then *Not reached: the tower. Do not narrate it.* or, after Fall back, *Fell back after the gate; the yard and the tower were not attempted.*; then the members' end state, key moments from each stage and, when a stage was won, the loot and XP.
- **Per reply:** a plan belongs to the reply it started from, like a fight since 0.4.3: delete that reply, swipe it or replace it, and the plan (between stages too) and its result are gone. While a plan is under way it counts as one battle: no story instruction, wounds or offers; the Battle tab stays; the hand-off waits and clears as a fight's does.
- **The story instruction** now tells `then:` (more foes join) from `then at <place>:` (the next fight, elsewhere): about 185 tokens (was 169; the test bound moved from 170 to 185).
- **Any place phrase starts a stage:** not only `then at <place>:`, but any short place before a colon after `then`: `then inside the den:`, `then into the cave:`, `then across the ford:`, a bare `then the tower:` (a prefix of one to six words with no digit). A leading *at* is dropped from the place's name (*the yard*); other words stay (*inside the den*). The same goes for the first group (`the old mill: 2 wolf`). `then:` with nothing before the colon is still a wave, and so is a `then …:` whose prefix cannot be a place (a digit, more than six words). A colon never ends up in a foe's name: any prefix left over is dropped from it (found live: *then inside the den: old dog wolf* was read as a wave of *Inside The Den: Old Dog Wolf*).
- **Fix: a boss's body parts drop once.** A boss rolled its whole loot table `rules.loot.bossRolls` (2) times, materials included, so one *Grey Wolf Matriarch* could drop *Grey Wolf Matriarch Pelt ×3*. Now materials (pelts, fangs, hides…) roll once per creature, boss or not, each within its own quantity and with the coinless first-material boost as before; the boss's extra rolls are for its equipment and consumables only; money is unchanged. A non-boss's drops and a boss's first roll are the same draws as before, so only a boss's later drops can come out differently for a seed.
- **Fix: one pelt per creature.** A material row's quantity could still give one creature two of a part it has one of (found live: three made-up young wolves dropped *Young Wolf Pelt ×4*). A whole-body part, by the material name's last word (pelt, hide, skin, head, heart, carapace, shell, horn, antler, tail, jaw, skull, eye, and their plurals, any case; `enemies.wholePart`), now drops at most one per creature; teeth, fangs, claws, feathers, scales and the like keep their quantities. The quantity is still drawn, so the drops after it stay the same for a seed. Shipped rows this caps: *Scrap Hide*, *Tough Hide*, *Fine Pelt* (rank tables) and *Rat Tail* were 1–2, now 1.
- **Fix:** a fight's result now keeps the full record of the reply it started from (place, send date, swipe); it fell back to the reply's place only.
- **Fix: a rolled-back fight's offer comes back.** A fought or put-away offer was remembered by its reply's place and text, so after deleting back past a fight and getting the identical reply again at the same place, the fight was never offered again (found live). Now each offer put away (Not now, or fought) remembers its reply (place, send date, swipe; `dismissedAt`), and once that reply is deleted, swiped away or replaced, the key no longer hides the new reply's offers; the reply a fight started from (`foughtAt`) likewise, so the new reply's other offers are not held back by a fight that never happened; and the result screen's record goes with its fight's reply. Keys saved before 0.5.0 carry no such record and hold as before.
- **Fix: a narrating reply never offers XP or a rest.** Only the reply the waiting hand-off was given to was treated as narrating; once that hand-off was spent and a later fight's came and was rolled back, the narrating reply was the latest again and offered the XP its fight had already given (found live: *XP +25: drove off the weir pack… Award?*). The replies that narrate a fight are now remembered (place, send date, swipe; `narrated`, recorded once the reply is in), and none of them offers XP or a rest; a reply deleted or swiped in that place is a new reply and narrates nothing unless it gets the hand-off.

## 0.4.3

- **A fight goes back with its reply.** A fight's result waited in the chat's metadata, tied to no reply. Delete back to before the fight (or swipe the reply it started from) and replay the scene, and the old result was still handed to the model with every reply: it narrated that fight again (the same loot, every time), and while a result waits RPG Battle asks for no `<Wounds>` or `<Encounter>`, so no new fight could be offered. Now a fight, and its result, belong to the reply that was the latest when the fight started (its place, send date and swipe). Deleted, swiped to another version or replaced, and both are gone: the next reply gets the story instruction again. Editing that reply keeps them, and a swipe of the reply that narrates the result still narrates the same fight.
- **Older chats:** a result saved before 0.4.3 has no such record; the reply the last fight started from (`foughtAt`) stands in, so deleting back past it clears the result too.
- The check runs whenever the state is read, and on a deleted or swiped message and on opening a chat.

## 0.4.2

The engagement and the stakes are the story's: you decide whether to fight, not what the fight is.

- **Read-only setup:** after **Fight?** the Battle tab shows the engagement as the story gave it (its `<Encounter>`, or the fight Story Director planned), resolved by code as before (the bestiary or a made-up creature, threat words to a power, boss, roles, `then:` engagements): per engagement, each line's count, name (tagged *new* when it will be made up), power in your terms, role and *Boss*, and the stakes as text. No inputs, no add or remove, no rank, role, boss, count or stakes controls; **Start** and **Not now**. *Preparing foes…* still shows while new creatures are flavoured. The setup's draft, row editors, power picker, autocomplete and stakes choice are gone.
- **Stakes from the story:** `<Encounter>3 bandit; then: bandit captain (boss) | to the death</Encounter>` (also `| death`, `| stakes: death`, any case) is a fight to the death; without a mark, normal. A Story Director planned fight's text may carry the same mark. What follows a bar is never read as a foe. The story instruction says so (`; | to the death if defeat is death`, about 9 tokens; now about 169, within the 170 bound). The offer and its toast say *To the death* when it is; the mark itself is never shown in the offer line.
- **The glue enforces it:** Start sends nothing and the fight is built only from the offer RPG Battle parsed (`story.storyFight`), so a changed page cannot change the foes or the stakes; without an offer there is no fight ("Fights come from the story"). Fight? likewise opens the offer it parsed, not rows sent back by the tab.
- **No Discard:** the result screen keeps **Return to story** and **Done**; a fight's result always reaches the story (Done leaves the hand-off waiting for the next reply, shown in the Party tab), so the sheets and the narration agree.
- **The hand-off clears once narrated:** the *A fight result is waiting* notice (and its Return to story) shows only until a reply is given the result; while that reply is being generated a quiet *being narrated* line shows, and once it is in, nothing. Its swipes still narrate the same result; a newer reply spends it at once; deleting the narrating reply makes it wait again for the next one. A narrated result no longer holds back the story's next fight offer (`handoff.handoffState`, `settleHandoff`).
- **Coin only from foes that carry it:** bandits, cultists, goblins, orcs and other people, their leaders and casters drop money as before (luck and the boss multiplier unchanged); beasts, insects, oozes, plants, constructs, spirits and the dead drop none, and a drake or wyvern only as a boss (its hoard). Instead their first material (a *Wolf Pelt*, *Spider Silk*, *Slime Jelly*, *Old Bone*…) is at least 85 % likely (`rules.loot.coinlessMaterial`), so most such kills leave something of the creature. The shipped bestiary marks each creature (`coins: true | false | 'boss'`); a made-up creature goes by its template (`enemies.carriesCoin`). A fight stored before 0.4.2 pays as it did. Loot is still seeded: a coin carrier's drops are unchanged; a coinless foe's item rolls may come out differently (a material that now drops draws its quantity).
- **Fix:** `rollLoot` called without a luck value used luck 0 (an unlucky party) instead of the start stat; fights always pass it, so fights are unaffected.

## 0.4.1

Mastery progress rolls back with the story; NPC renames are followed.

- **Mastery progress in the Party block:** each skill on the Skills (and Set aside) line carries its uses toward the next mastery level, `Power Strike (Lv 2, 3/12)`, and `(Lv 5, max)` at the top, for your section and each companion's. A fight's result writes it with the rest of the sheet, so deleting, swiping or editing the reply takes it back like HP and XP (it used to be kept in chat metadata, out of the block, and survived a deleted reply). Read leniently: `Name Lv 2 (3/12)`, `(3 of 12 uses)`, commas inside the brackets; a line without progress (`Power Strike (Lv 2)`, as older blocks have) reads as none yet. An older chat's progress from metadata fills in the skills its block gives none, is written into the latest reply the first time the chat is open (or with the next change to the sheets), and the metadata record is then dropped and never written again. A rename no longer has progress to move.
- **NPC renames followed:** a companion linked to an NPC State Delta NPC takes the NPC's new name on its own, with a notice (*Lyra is now Lyra Vance (from NPC State Delta).*), written into the latest reply like any sheet change (deleting it takes the rename back), once per change (the NPC's last-seen name is kept per chat, so a deleted reply or a name changed back by hand stays). Looked at when the Party tab draws, after each reply and when the suite changes; not during a fight. The *Rename?* offer for NPC renames is gone (a rename put away as an offer before is not followed); the persona rename offer stays. A renamed companion's NPC link is kept under the old id too, so a deleted reply leaves them linked.

## 0.4.0

The story drives, the reader decides: fights, rests, skills, companions and XP come from the
story as offers; there is no free fight, rest or skill on demand.

- **No back doors:** a skill is learned only from a waiting offer and under the story's name (its type, stat, target and element can be adjusted; the library is not on offer beyond that skill's own entry); a companion is added only from a `<Joins>` offer, at the story's level (or at most yours), with the skills worked out for them, and no gear typed in.

- **One Party block:** your character is its first section (`## Ren (you)`, with the level line in your terms, HP/MP, stats, Checks, State, Skills, Conditions), the companions follow (with their Equipped line); one prompt note covers both and the Dice modifier reads your section's Checks line. Older chats' `<Character>` + `<Party>` (and a Party block without a "(you)" section) are still read; the chat's next write (on opening it) makes them one, and the old Character block is no longer kept, prompted or given a tab (registered transport-only, so its old copies stay hidden). Registered with `tab: false` on Story Block 0.9.1 or newer (older: its tab as before). `api.members()` keeps its shape.
- **Two tabs:** **Party**, always there (the party as cards, yours first and open, companions folded to name, level, HP/MP and State; open: stats with checks for you, skills in use and set aside, gear, controls; which are open is kept per chat), and **Battle**, only while there is a battle (the story's offer, the setup, the fight, the result until Return to story or Done). No Start fight, Learn skill, Add companion, Short rest or Long rest buttons.
- **Creation is stats only:** name (from your persona) and the starting points; no presets, no starter skills (a character without skills still attacks, defends, uses items, parleys and flees).
- **Offers from the story:** one combined instruction (about 160 tokens; replaces the wounds/encounter one) asks for `<Joins>`, `<Leaves>`, `<Learned>`, `<XP>minor|major|milestone: why</XP>` and `<Rest>short|long: why</Rest>` besides `<Wounds>` and `<Encounter>`, each only when the story makes it true; Story Director 0.8.2's quests closed in a reply (`questsDoneAt`) become XP offers by difficulty. All transport-only blocks; offers show as toasts and at the top of the Party tab with Accept / Not now, never applied on their own, and only while their reply is the latest and no fight has started since it (a rest from before a fight never heals after it; a quest's XP likewise); a swipe recomputes them and one put away stays away for that reply's text. Names match by full or first name; unknown names are ignored except in Joins.
- **The opening message** (the greeting shown, any swipe) is read like a reply: its `<Learned>` gives your starting skills (optional hints `| type, stat, target, effect`; no stat requirements), its `<Joins>` brings companions (`| level, focus, skills`). Offers for your character wait until it is made or imported; its skills and companions wait until taken or put away, anything else in it lasts like a reply's.
- **Learned:** opens the learn form prefilled (type, stat, target and an element, fire/ice/lightning/poison, guessed from the words, editable); code sets power, MP and cooldown; a library skill keeps its requirements (the form says why).
- **Skill cap:** `rules.character.activeSkills` 6 (setting 3–12): more skills are kept, set aside (a `Set aside:` line), not used in fights, swapped on the card; learning past the cap asks which sits out; older sheets keep their first six.
- **Companions:** from a Joins offer, the Add companion form opens filled in: name, free role, level (clamped), control, gear, up to three **focus stats** (weights 3/2/1, prefilled from generic role words; none: even), every point spread by largest remainder. Their **skills** come from one words-only model call (the flavour settings, now the **Generated words** fold; code builds each skill at their level), with Generate again and a remove per skill; library skills fitting focus and role when the call is off or fails; a `| skills:` hint names them. With NPC State Delta 1.2.2, "From the story's NPCs" (`npcProfiles()`) fills in name, role and focus and keeps the link (a later rename there is offered), and **Dossier** on the card opens it (`openDossier`).
- **Growth:** companions on auto spend new points in their build's proportions (setting *Spend companions' points automatically*, on); commanded ones keep them for **Spend** (any member's). XP awards are a share of the member's next level (`xpAwards` 10/25/50 %, `questXp` 5/10/15/25 %, rules.json), each at their own level, none at the cap, through the fight's level-up path.
- **Rests from the story:** a `<Rest>` offer applies a short or long rest to the party; no double counting with story-time recovery in the same reply (the larger of the two; a long rest makes it moot). Not during a fight or while a result waits to be narrated.
- **Engagements back to back:** `<Encounter>3 bandit; then: bandit captain (boss)</Encounter>` is one battle with waves joining as the field clears (HP, MP and conditions carry over), one result and one hand-off.
- **Give item / Take back** between your Inventory's equipment rows and a companion's gear (slot limits and rarity caps apply to them).
- **Death:** a companion at 0 HP after a fight to the death is offered for removal (it asks twice); losing a fight to the death leaves `State: dead` on your sheet, a game-over notice with Start over, and a plain hand-off ("Ren is dead and does not come back").
- **Start over** (two clicks, *Keep companions*), **party export/import** (JSON, cleaned; your file gear into the Inventory unless already there), **persona rename** offered once per switch.
- **Story Suite:** `api.manage.tokens()` estimates what RPG Battle adds to the next ordinary reply (the story instruction, or a waiting hand-off; about four characters a token; a 20-token allowance for the hidden blocks; the Party block is Story Block's to count). The settings panel wears the shared Story Suite look (`src/suite.css`, loaded once as `<link id="story-suite-css">`); new **Party** fold (skills in use, auto-spend); the Character tag is gone.

## 0.3.1

Equipment limits you can see.

- **Worn pieces:** helms, hoods, gloves, gauntlets, bracers, boots, greaves and belts are a slot of their own (`worn`; from the name, a category like `[Equipment > Boots]`, or `slot: worn`), so they no longer take the two accessory places from rings and amulets. A helm or boots in `[Equipment > Armour]` is a worn piece, not the body armour. Singular names count too ("Leather Gauntlet").
- **How many count:** one worn piece and two accessories by default; **Settings → Equipment** sets each from 0 to 9 (`rules.gear`). Weapon, off-hand and armour stay one each.
- **Gear in the Battle tab:** what the fight uses, with its stats as capped by rarity ("STR +2 · capped from STR +20"), and a *Not counted* line for anything else marked equipped, with why ("only 2 accessories count", "one weapon counts", "Greatsword is two-handed").

## 0.3.0

The story and the numbers agree.

- **State line** on the Character block and each Party section: HP, MP and conditions in words the model must portray ("badly hurt (bleeding, slow to move) · drained of magic · poisoned"), worked out by code with bands in `rules.json` (`narrative`) and never read back. The blocks' prompt notes say to portray it exactly and never contradict it, and that the scene's Visible Condition must agree. The hand-off ends each member's line with the same words, and the Battle tab shows them under the HP and MP bars.
- **Story wounds:** a short instruction (about 90 tokens, its own prompt key, while a Character block exists) asks the model for a hidden `<Wounds>` block when the story itself hurts or heals someone on the sheets: `Name — scratch|wound|severe|grievous: cause`, `Name — heal <word>: cause`, `Name — poisoned: cause`, `Name — cure poison: cause`. Code turns the word into a share of max HP (`rules.json` → `wounds`: 10 / 30 / 50 / 80 %); harm can down someone, healing stops at max, one harm-or-heal line per person per reply, names matched by full or first name. Applied after story-time recovery, into that reply, from the sheets before it (swipes recompute, continues do not double), never during or narrating a fight; a reply whose sheets were changed since (a rest, an item, a fight) is left alone.
- **Undo** for story wounds, in a toast and in the Battle tab, while that reply's sheets are still the ones it wrote; undone wounds stay undone for that reply's text.
- **Fights from the story:** the model names a fight that breaks out in a hidden `<Encounter>` block (`3 bandit, bandit captain (boss)`) and stops at the first blow; a toast and the Battle tab offer **Fight?** (the setup opens with the rows filled in, still editable) or **Not now**. With Story Director 0.8, its planned fights that are due or due soon (`StoryDirector.encountersAt`) are offered the same way.
- **Story Block's Character Sheet** block on beside RPG Battle is pointed out in the Battle tab and the settings ("two HP values will disagree"), with a **Turn it off** button; it is never turned off on its own.
- **Story Suite handshake** ([docs/STORY_SUITE.md](docs/STORY_SUITE.md); `src/suite.js`, the same file in Story Block, Story Director and NPC State Delta): while it is on, RPG Battle announces itself as `rpg-battle` owning `stats` and `rolls`, and `items` after Story Block's Inventory, and is withdrawn when switched off. `api.members()` shares the party: the Character and each companion as frozen copies `{ id, name, role, level, levelLabel, hp: { cur, max }, mp: { cur, max }, state }` from the kept sheets (`[]` without one). The Character Sheet notice now follows the suite: shown while Story Block offers `stats` and RPG Battle is the source of truth for them; a Story Block that does not join the suite is read from its settings as before. **Settings → Source of truth**: Automatic, This extension or The other one for stats, roll modifiers and items (`sourceOfTruth`, default Automatic), with who the story follows now; alone, nothing changes.
- `<Wounds>` and `<Encounter>` are registered as transport-only Story Block blocks, so they never show in the narration.
- **Your world's terms** (Settings → Terms): a level name, an XP name, optional tier titles (`size` levels per tier, Roman or Arabic numerals: levels 1–10 are "Soul Class I"…) and a level cap. The Character block and Party sections write the level line in them (`Soul Class: III (Lv 24) | Soul Growth: 28.10/100 | Points: 2`, or `Soul Power: 24 | Power Growth: 5400/11760 | Points: 2`) and read back the canonical form, any terms form (also without the colon) and older blocks; with tiers, progress is the share through the current tier. The Battle tab, the result screen and the hand-off's XP line (`Soul Growth +12%`, `Power Growth +38`) use them, and the Character block's Story Block fields are registered under them (again when they change, and the sheets are rewritten in the new words).
- **Level cap:** no level past it, XP stops there and shows MAX (also with the plain terms, at 99).
- **Enemy power in your terms:** with terms of your own, the setup's rank picker offers your tiers (or levels) up to the cap instead of F–SSS; a picked level is placed on the tuned rank table by interpolating between the two ranks that bracket it (`rankRowForLevel`, `projectEnemy({ level })`), and enemy cards show it in your terms. `<Encounter>` may name a power in brackets (`3 wolf (Soul Class I)`, `bandit captain (boss, Soul Class II)`, `(level 12)`). F–SSS work as before with the plain terms.
- **Edit companion:** a companion's name, role and control, from their row in the Battle tab; sheet, skills and gear stay, and their mastery progress moves with them when the rename changes their id.
- **Threat words in an encounter:** `<Encounter>3 marsh wolf (weak), wolf matriarch (boss, strong)</Encounter>`: trivial, weak, even, strong, deadly (and synonyms: minor, lesser, tough, dangerous, lethal, elite…), in brackets or, for the unmistakable ones, before the name. They are relative to the party: the Character's level plus `rules.json` → `threat.offsets` steps (−2, −1, 0, +1, +2), a step being one tier with tier titles on, else `threat.step` (4) levels, within 1 and the cap. A rank or level written outright wins. The setup shows the word beside the power it became. The story instruction mentions them (about 108 tokens at four characters a token, from 92; the test bound moved from 95 to 110).
- **Plurals of creature words** are made singular in an encounter ("3 bog wights" → three Bog Wights), so a made-up creature is not named as a plural.
- **Monsters on the fly:** a foe neither bestiary knows is made up when its fight starts, from a template its name's words pick (beast, undead, humanoid, leader, caster, construct, ooze, insect, drake, spirit, plant, or a fallback): a role, traits that move its numbers within bounds (`rules.json` → `generated.traits`, product within 0.6–1.5), whether it talks, a temperament (morale, when it flees), two or three skills of its kind built by the skill machinery at the level it is fought at, and a loot table of its kind with gear rarity from its power. Deterministic per name, level and chat. A balance test holds an even fight against made-up foes near one against projected ones.
- **Flavour** (on by default): one model call per new creature (at most four per fight, 30 seconds for all), on the chat's model or a connection profile, asks for words only (look, traits, talks, temperament, skill names with a few words, loot names with a rarity and a stat); code maps the words onto the template's knobs, renames skills and loot, drops every number and every unknown word, and caps rarity one step above the creature's. Failure, timeout or no model: the template creature, with a console note. *Preparing foes…* in the setup meanwhile. Settings → Monsters: on/off, model, answer length.
- **The chat's bestiary:** made-up creatures are kept in the chat metadata (`rpgBattle.bestiary`, cleaned on every store), so a name is the same creature for the rest of the story; names are looked up there first, then in the shipped bestiary, then made up. The setup tags unknown names **new**, so do the cards of creatures made for this fight; a **Bestiary** fold on the Battle tab lists this story's creatures (power in the terms, role, traits, look) with **Regenerate**, **Forget**, **Export** and **Import** (JSON, cleaned on the way in).

## 0.2.0

Recovery and companions.

- **Recovery as story time passes** (with Story Director 0.7.2+): after each reply, 5 % of max HP and MP per story hour since the reply before, and a full rest after 6 hours or more; written into that reply (swipes and edits recompute it), with a note in the Battle tab. Switchable in the settings.
- **Short rest** (a quarter back) and **Long rest** (full, fight conditions end) replace Rest.
- **Use item** out of a fight: heals, MP and cures from the Inventory block, on you or a companion, taken off the inventory; damage items stay for fights.
- **Add companion**: name, role, level, build (preset), control and optional equipment; companions join the Party block, fight beside you, and can be switched between auto and command or removed (two clicks).

## 0.1.0

The first release.

- **Character**: no classes; seven stats from 5 with 10 points to spend, three starter skills (five optional presets), a Character block kept by RPG Battle (Story Block 0.7) that the model reads every reply and never writes, with a Checks line the Dice block uses as its modifier.
- **Levels and points** you spend yourself; **skills** learned by hand from the library (with stat requirements) or from a template, with mastery that grows with use; **Rest**.
- **Fights resolved in code**: Start fight with any enemy names (a 20-creature bestiary, ranks F to SSS, five roles, bosses), turn order, hits, criticals, damage, defend, conditions, cooldowns, items, parley and flee; enemies and auto companions by predictable rules; **Auto**; waves in the engine. Seeded, stored as setup and actions, replayed exactly after a reload.
- **Equipment** from the Inventory block, its stats capped by rarity and level; **consumables** used in fights; **loot and money** written back into the inventory, money in copper/silver/gold, one coin, or your own currency.
- **The hand-off**: one short summary injected into the next reply only (its swipes narrate the same result), with *Return to story*.
- **Party** block for companions on auto or command.
- Balance tuned by a simulation in the test suite: an even solo fight lasts about 5 rounds and costs about half your HP.
